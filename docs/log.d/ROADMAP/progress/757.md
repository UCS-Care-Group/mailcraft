| 2026-11-03 | HTML mail: tables side by side, and frames drawn with the lines of cells ([#757](https://github.com/UCS-Care-Group/mailcraft/issues/757)). Two or more tables floated with `align="left"` or `align="right"` that are all their holder holds stand on one line, as a browser and Outlook float them: the left ones from the left edge, the right ones from the right edge, and one with no room left goes on below (a bill's label at the left and its value at the right; a greeting beside a notice). Where text or anything else stands beside them they stay as before, as text does not flow round them. An empty column of a layout table is dropped only when it is empty in every row, a cell spanning columns counted, so a frame drawn with the lines of cells keeps its heading inside it; and a layout table is not stacked for the narrowness of columns that only make room. Tables laid out as boxes by `display: inline-block` (#597) now stand in one line too: each stood on a line of its own. The text of a table inside a centred cell starts at the left, as in a browser: the `align` of a cell, a row, a `<div>` or a `<p>` and a `<center>` stop at a table inside them, while a style's `text-align` and the `align` of a heading, a list or the body still reach in (as in a message with a doctype); the nested table itself still stands where they put it, a `<th>` in it is centred, and words written between its rows stand before it in the alignment round it. A wrapper a share of the line wide, or one round tables floated to a side, stays a table there and stands where that alignment says (a column 80 % wide in the middle, a label and its value together). Tables floated side by side stand as wide as they are drawn, so one whose words need more than its share no longer lies under the next. A cell that holds only pictures narrower than 24 px is as wide as they are, so a centred row of small icons stays in the middle. Tests in `crates/html/src/float_tests.rs`, `nested_align_tests.rs`, `blocks/tests.rs` and `reading/tests.rs` |
