| 2026-11-05 | #580, part 4: a picture moves by cut and paste (Ctrl+X and Ctrl+V, or Cut and Paste on a right click) within a message and within the open signature. A copy names each of the message's own pictures (or the open signature's) by a `cid:` made of its id and a hash of its bytes, never the bytes themselves, and says it in words as its description in brackets, so a picture cut alone is never an empty text; a paste shows a picture only when it is one the message or the signature holds now, with the same bytes, as wide and described as it has it; a paste or a new picture in the signature put in a message is refused, as it goes out with its own pictures only (before, a new picture there went out as an attachment and was missing from the text). Each time the text shows a picture counts toward what it may hold (30 in a message, 10 in a signature), less what it takes the place of, for a paste, a new picture and a draft reopened alike (a new picture over a selected one takes its place while there is room; at the cap it is refused, like any new picture), so pictures cut together always paste back together, and a paste that would show more is refused rather than cut short. Anything else is left out as before, so pasted HTML can at most show again a picture the owner already has there, once for each `<img>` that names it where the reading pane shows it (an OBJECT character, as it is or as an entity, is never one). A signature's picture in a message is not taken along (it goes out only in its signature). Pasted into another program, a copied picture shows as a frame with its description; into a plain-text message, its description. In the browser, as before, the clipboard carries text. A signature lets go of pictures its text no longer holds when a picture is added or it is saved, so one cut and pasted after that has to be added again.
