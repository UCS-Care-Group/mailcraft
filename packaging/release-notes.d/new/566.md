- **Rules kept on a Microsoft account's server.** A rule you make for a Microsoft account can be kept on its server, where it runs whether MailCraft is open or not and shows in Outlook on the web and on your phone: Keep on the server is ticked when you start a rule, and you can untick it to keep the rule on this computer. The rules made there show in MailCraft too. The Rules window shows one account's rules at a time, the server's apart from this computer's, says where each is kept and why, and has arrows to move a rule up or down and a button that moves a rule from this computer to the server. The first time a rule is to go there, MailCraft asks before Microsoft's page asks for permission to change your mailbox settings; until you allow it, your rules stay on this computer and nothing of them is sent. On a Microsoft account, "From contains" and "Recipient contains" look in the sender's and recipients' addresses alone, as the server does. When the server can't do a rule (flagging, a move to a folder of another account, a person's name rather than an address), the box is greyed and says why, and the rule stays on this computer. Always Move Messages From This Sender opens the new rule filled in, so you see it and where it is kept before you add it, and then runs that rule alone over the folder. A rule made in Outlook with parts MailCraft doesn't know can be switched on or off, moved or deleted, and is never rewritten ([#566](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/566)).
- **Rules: more of Outlook's conditions, exceptions and actions.** A rule can now look for mail from or sent to particular people, sent only to you, with your address in To or Cc, words in the subject or body or a header, a sensitivity, a size, a meeting invitation, an automatic reply, a category, a flag or the days it came, with several words or addresses in one, set apart by semicolons. Each of them can also be an exception (Except if), and a rule can copy mail to a folder, delete it for good, flag it with a due day, clear its flag or its categories, and show a desktop alert for the mail it acts on, even when alerts for new mail are off. On a Microsoft account, a rule the server can do is kept there; one that deletes for good, looks at the days mail came, flags mail, clears a flag or categories, copies mail out of the mailbox or shows an alert stays on this computer. On a Microsoft or Exchange server account, a rule on this computer that looks at the text of new mail, a header, its size or whether it is a meeting invitation acts once the message is downloaded whole ([#566](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/566)).
