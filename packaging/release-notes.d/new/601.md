- **Pictures behind the text of designed mail.** A newsletter or a piece of stationery that puts a picture behind a table, a cell or the whole message (a banner photograph with a headline on it, a paper texture, a gradient) now shows it, at the size and place it asks for, once or repeated. A picture that is attached to the message or written into it is shown straight away; one on the web is never downloaded for a background, whatever you allow (only the message's pictures proper are), so it cannot tell its sender the message was read. Where the picture is shown as it is over a colour the sender gave the box, the text colours the sender wrote there are kept (they are meant for the picture, which is not looked at, not for the colour under it, so white text over a dark banner stays white); where the sender gave no colour, the picture is softened so its text stays readable, and in the dark theme the picture is dimmed and its text is made readable against the colour turned dark, as before. Dark or default-coloured text over a dark picture is not changed: the sender's text colour is trusted, and the program does not look at the picture's colours. With high contrast on, the picture is left out. Nothing hidden in the message, and no picture behind a box of a pixel or two, is shown ([#601](https://github.com/GITHUB_REPOSITORY/issues/601)).
